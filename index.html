About MeteoOnto

A semantic foundation for interoperable meteorological data and knowledge.

MeteoOnto provides a structured semantic representation of the meteorological domain. It captures important concepts, relationships, observations, measurements, and forecasts used to describe atmospheric and weather-related phenomena.

The ontology is intended to support knowledge representation, semantic data integration, interoperability, discovery, and reasoning across heterogeneous meteorological datasets.

Goal: Enable machines and applications to understand and connect meteorological information using formal semantic relationships.
🌍

Why an Ontology?

Meteorological information is produced by many different systems, organizations, sensors, and data formats. A shared ontology provides common concepts and relationships that enable these sources to communicate semantically.

Core Ontology Concepts

MeteoOnto organizes meteorological knowledge into interconnected semantic concepts.

🌦️

Weather Phenomena

Representation of phenomena such as rain, snow, fog, thunderstorms, clouds, wind, and other atmospheric events.

🌡️

Atmospheric Conditions

Concepts describing temperature, pressure, humidity, visibility, wind conditions, and related atmospheric states.

📡

Observations

Semantic representation of observations collected from meteorological instruments and observation systems.

🛰️

Sensors

Representation of sensors, platforms, instruments, measurements, and their relationships to observations.

🔮

Forecasts

Concepts for representing predicted atmospheric conditions, forecast periods, locations, and forecast variables.

📍

Spatial & Temporal Context

Representation of locations, regions, time periods, observation times, and forecast validity periods.

Potential Applications

MeteoOnto can provide a semantic foundation for meteorological and environmental applications.

🔗

Data Integration

Connect meteorological datasets originating from different systems and organizations.

🧠

Knowledge Graphs

Build machine-readable knowledge graphs representing meteorological entities and their relationships.

🔎

Semantic Search

Discover meteorological information based on meaning, relationships, location, time, and context.

📊

Data Analytics

Provide a common semantic structure for analytical and decision-support systems.

🌐

Interoperability

Improve communication between heterogeneous meteorological systems and data providers.

🤖

Semantic Reasoning

Support automated reasoning over meteorological observations, phenomena, and relationships.

Semantic Representation Example

An example of how a meteorological observation can be expressed using semantic technologies.
