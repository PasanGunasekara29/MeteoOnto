1. Development

Mete∞nto models meteorological knowledge across observations, sensors and platforms, measurements, forecasts, datasets, places, time, extreme weather, and risk. Its modular design brings these connected domains into a shared semantic model while reusing established vocabularies where appropriate.

Development strategy: NeOn methodology

The development is organized with the NeOn methodology as a guiding framework for building ontology networks. NeOn emphasizes specifying requirements, planning development activities, reusing and re-engineering existing ontological and non-ontological resources, and evaluating the resulting model. For Mete∞nto, this provides a lifecycle structure for turning meteorological knowledge needs into modular OWL/RDF representations and documenting how external vocabularies are reused and aligned.

The NeOn framework is scenario-based: the concrete process can combine activities according to available resources and project needs. Accordingly, the sections below describe Mete∞nto’s repository artifacts and documentation; they do not claim unreported tools, reasoner results, or validation outcomes.

Ontology modeling approach

Development turns the ontology’s scope and competency questions into OWL classes, properties, and axioms. The Turtle files use the following core modeling ideas.

◇

Classes and hierarchy

Classes represent categories of meteorological entities. rdfs:subClassOf states that every instance of a specialized class is also an instance of its parent. The files also use equivalent and disjoint classes, unions, complements, and restrictions to express class meaning and organization.

↔

Properties and logical constraints

Object properties connect resources; datatype properties connect resources to typed values; annotation properties provide labels and comments. Domain and range axioms describe expected endpoints. Subproperties, equivalent and inverse properties, property chains, functional, symmetric, transitive and irreflexive characteristics, and existential or universal restrictions add relationship semantics.

⌘

Modules, data, and interoperability

Core terms are extended through forecasting, geospatial, extreme-weather, and risk modules. Individuals and XML Schema datatypes represent examples and literal values; annotations, imports, and version metadata document the files. Alignment axioms connect MeteoOnto terms to established vocabularies.

OWL follows an open-world assumption: an unstated fact is unknown, not automatically false. A reasoner can check consistency and infer class relationships from axioms; report any reasoner, version, and results in the evaluation section.

2. Requirements

Mete∞nto requirements focus on connecting weather measurements to the context needed to interpret and reuse them.

  • Represent observations, observed properties, and their results.
  • Connect measurements to sensors, platforms, deployments, and procedures.
  • Describe dataset and provenance context.
  • Represent spatial places and geometry, along with instants, intervals, and temporal resolution.
  • Extend the core model to forecasts, extreme weather, and risk.

These requirements are reflected in the competency questions listed in Development and in the ontology’s modular structure.

3. Implementation

Mete∞nto is implemented as an OWL/RDF ontology and published in Turtle format. The repository provides the core model and modules for forecasting, geospatial knowledge, extreme weather, and risk and safety.

Separate alignment files connect Mete∞nto concepts to SOSA/SSN, QUDT, PROV-O, OWL-Time, GeoSPARQL, DBpedia, and SWEET. These mappings support interoperability while retaining Mete∞nto’s domain-specific terms.
